# Founder Notes - 2026-07-09

Today clarified the product thesis.

Seneschal is not primarily an AI governance product. AI agents matter, but they
are one producer of operational intent among many: deployment pipelines,
infrastructure automation, MCP servers, platform workflows, internal services,
and human operators all attempt to execute capabilities on behalf of the
organization.

The durable thesis is Capability Governance.

Capabilities are becoming first-class governance objects. The important
question is not only who the caller is, or what resource they can access, but
whether a capability should execute under the current circumstances.

The architecture is settling into complementary layers:

- Authentication answers: who are you?
- Authorization answers: what can you access?
- Capability governance answers: should this capability execute now, in this
  context, for this identity, against this resource?
- Execution performs the action.
- Observability records and explains what happened.

Seneschal should sit between authorization and execution. It should extend
identity, authorization, and observability systems rather than replacing them.

Runtime governance mode became a control-plane concept today. `LogOnly` and
`Enforce` are not just implementation flags; they represent the organization's
current governance posture. `LogOnly` supports observation and readiness work.
`Enforce` turns decisions into operational consequences. This needs to be
visible, understandable, and eventually auditable.

Trusted integrations also became more concrete. If applications, middleware,
CLIs, agents, and automation systems call Seneschal for decisions, they need a
simple trust boundary. Scoped integration API keys are a V1 mechanism for that:
they authenticate integrations and constrain which identities and capabilities
an integration may request decisions for.

Dynamic organizational context matters. The same capability may be acceptable
or unacceptable depending on deployment freezes, incidents, maintenance
windows, approvals, ownership, risk level, and current runtime activity. This
context is where capability governance becomes more than static policy
matching.

The product should continue moving away from "AI at the center" and toward
"operational intent at the center." AI agents are important because they
increase the volume and autonomy of operational intent, but the governance
problem exists across the whole enterprise operating model.

The near-term product direction should stay grounded:

- Make capabilities first-class governance objects.
- Make runtime decisions explainable.
- Keep observation before enforcement.
- Treat governance mode as a visible control-plane setting.
- Keep integrations simple enough that platform engineers can adopt them
  quickly.
- Preserve a clean boundary between decision, execution, audit, and
  observability.

This is the shape of Seneschal: a capability governance layer for modern
software operations.
